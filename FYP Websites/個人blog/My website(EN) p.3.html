My Thoughts

Why Five Taste?

I first heard about Five Taste in my first year at university. Some senior students told me it was a legendary spot for anyone living in the hostel. They said that after all-night studying, drinking, or playing mahjong, folks would gather here for breakfast before going back to bed. When they took me to try it, I was amazed by the taste of satay beef noodles and the portion size of the breakfast set

In the following years, I would go to Five Taste whenever I had a stressful, sleepless night or sometimes for no reason at all. I would always order the satay beef noodles and a pork cutlet. This restaurant, which is opens at 6:30 AM has been accompanied me through tough mornings and become a part of my university life.

So when I had to choose a shop for the project, Five Taste was the first place that came into my mind. Of course, the owner said yes without hesitation which made the choice even easier.

How I Gathered the Information

The material for this project came from a few different sources:

1. Talking to the owner

I asked him questions via WhatsApp, searched information online, and used the most direct method — going to the shop to talk to him. Sometimes I sent questions on WhatsApp, the owner was busy and wouldn't reply until the next day. Later I learned to ask him, "When are you working? I'll come to talk to you at the shop." In ten minutes, I could solve a whole week's questions.

2. On site inspection

Sometimes I sat in the shop to eat breakfast. While breakfast time, I paid attention to who came in, what they ordered, and how the staff chatted with the customers.

My Reflections on This Project

This project felt very different from the translations I worked on in the past. The assignments usually came with an original text, and my only focus was how to translate it well. This time I started from nothing. No original text, no template and no fixed format. I had to make every decision by myself. Thinking about what to write, how to write and what kind of tone I should use.

I realised that working on a project like this requires a lot more skills than I expected. I had to think about how to make the website look good. I also kept asking myself, "Will others understand what I'm writing? Can they feel it?" These are questions I never thought about in my translation assignments.

There was also something else I didn't expect: the owner is very talkative.I thought he would be busy and cool. But once we sat down and started chatting, he were willing to talk about everything. Like why he opened the restaurant, how he got scolded as apprentices, why the restaurant don't serve dinner... These were all true stories. The kind of stories you can't make up.

In the end, these stories became the words and sentences on this website. Translation is not just about changing words from one language to another. It is about turning someone's words, a shop's story, and a person's hard work into writing. Letting more people to see the stories.

Challenges

Of course, everything didn't go smoothly. I think the biggest challenge was communication efficiency. As university student, I usually work at night and wake up late. He runs a shop that opens early in the morning and closes in the late afternoon. Our daily routines just didn't match. Many times, I would think of a question at night and send him a message. I had to wait until the next day for a reply. Sometimes the owner was too busy and didn't reply at all. It was frustrating to wait for a response

Eventually, I realised that I needed to take the initiative and adjust my way of communication to fit his rhythm. Instead of waiting passively for reply, I asked when he would be available and called him on phone. Later on, I already knew when the owner would be free, so I just called him directly.

Another challenge was that there was no previous example to follow. I was the first person in this department doing this project. At least among my friends I know, no one had ever done that before. There were no past examples to refer to. Also, the guidelines for the Corporate Communication project are quite broad. I often had to figure things out as I kept working on the project. That feeling of having nothing to follow was quite unsettling at first.

Gains

When I look back on this project, I think the most valuable thing I learnt was not about the translating or knowing how to build a website. It was about how to communicate with people and earn their trust.

At first, I thought the biggest challenge would be technical or translation itself. But later, I realised that the real challenge was already here from the very beginning. I wondered why the owner would want to help me. The truth is, he had no obligation to assist a student's project. He open the shop at 6:30 every morning and work straight through until late afternoon. He is exhausted enough after a whole day's work. How could he have time to answer all my questions?

So, I decided think about it in a “win-win” situation. I walked into Five Taste to introduced myself and explained that I was working on my final year project. I wanted to create a website to promote his shop. Since I have been eating there for several years. I think the food is delicious and have a special connection to this place. If he could spare some time to talk to me and give me the information I needed, I would try my best to build a website for him. That way he would get some advertising, and I would be able to complete my project and graduate. This arrangement would benefit everyone.Fortunately, I was able to make a deal with the owner when we first met. Although I did not see him during my first visit, I asked the staff on duty when he would be around. On my second visit, I met him without any trouble. The whole process took about a week.

This experience taught me that in the world of corporate communication, 'sincerity' and 'mutual benefit' are the best icebreakers. If you want to make a deal, you must create an outcome that satisfies both parties. Then you can achieve twice the result with half the effort. Perhaps that is what makes this project unique.
