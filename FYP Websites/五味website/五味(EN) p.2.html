📖 The Five Taste Files

We are a Hong Kong-style cha chaan teng with two locations in Tuen Mun: Hung Kiu branch (our first branch, established in 2014) and Lam Tei branch (opened in 2021). We operate only until the afternoon and do not serve dinner.

Both are named Five Taste. We operate only until the afternoon and do not serve dinner.

Signboard of Hung Kiu Branch
Signboard of Lam Tei Branch

A cha chaan teng (Hong Kong-style cafe) is an inexpensive restaurant that combines Chinese and Western cuisine. Each cha chaan teng has its own signature dishes. Pineapple buns and egg tarts are popular dishes. However, we don't serve these at Five Taste. Our must-try dishes are deep-fried pork chop cutlet, pan-fried pork chop, and satay beef noodles. We serve quickly, offer affordable prices, and are loved by our customers in the neighbourhood.

🤝 Five Tastes, Five Men

Five Taste is more than just an ordinary cha chaan teng. It was born from the dream of five of us who worked hard together in a kitchen.

Back then, we worked in the same kitchen of another restaurant for many years. We often got scolded and yelled at by the chefs which was truly tough. By chance, we all shared the same interest in opening our own restaurant. Why make money for others instead of fighting for ourselves?

The name "Five Taste" stands for the five of us — five different tastes. As time passed, now two of us — Boss Choy and Boss Leung — continue to run our business. But our care and persistence have never changed.

(Left) Boss Leung, (Right) Boss Choy

🫂 What the Kitchen Taught Us

"When I was an apprentice, I got scolded by the chef all the time. Foul words. I heard every nasty thing you could imagine."

These words come from our own experience. Because of that, we learned that we should never treat others that way — whether they are staff or customers.

We believe in humane management. Be a little nicer to your staff. Be a little more honest with your customers. Respect each other.

From scolded apprentices to bosses ourselves, we have learned: "We can't treat people the way we were treated back then. We can't be that aggressive anymore."
